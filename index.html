Turn on the microphone and play a note: the dot shows how far off it is, and its trail scrolls down behind the instrument. Tap a string’s note to lock the tuner to it and hear it (tap again to let go); ▲ ▼ beside it retune it by a semitone. The picker under the headstock sets the number of strings and the tuning.

A catalogue of exercises: pick one and press Play, or the space bar; it loops until you pause it, and dragging across bars loops just those. Start slow, and raise the tempo only when it’s clean. Set the tempo on the metronome by sliding its weight, with − and +, or with its 50, 75 and 100% keys (of the exercise’s starting tempo); its knob sets the click (0 silences it) and its COUNT switch counts in a bar. Scales and modes come in positions along the neck. Rhythm is a course from counting sixteenths to odd and mixed meters; the tab shows each pick stroke. Voice, the second instrument at the top, is for singing: see your pitch as a line, find your range, sing exercises set in it, and see how far to move a song to fit your voice.

Add a Guitar Pro (3–8) or MusicXML file with the card at the top of your files, or drop it there; it stays in this browser. Play it, slow it down with the metronome’s 50, 75 and 100% keys or its weight, and drag across bars to loop them (a click clears the loop). Keys: space plays and pauses, ← and → step a bar, Home or a click on the time goes back to the start, F fills the screen. Mix sets the guitar, vocals and backing (also on the amp). Pick a vocal track to sing along: turn on the microphone in the Sing bar and it scores each pass against the notes (an octave either way counts); turn the vocals down, or use headphones. The pencil turns on edit mode for guitar tracks: click a string position, type 0–9 for a fret, ↑ ↓ change string, ← → move between beats, Delete removes the note (Shift+Delete the beat), Insert adds a beat, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo and redo. Then save a copy, or download the file.

      Before you sing

      • Warm up for five minutes: hum, lip trills (“brrr”), and slow sirens from low to high.
      • Breathe low, into your belly, and let the air out evenly; your shoulders stay down.
      • Fifteen to twenty minutes a day beats an hour once a week. Drink water.
      • Stop if your throat hurts or your voice goes hoarse. Leave rasp and screams until your clean voice is steady, and learn them from a teacher.
      • Record yourself: you sound different from outside your head, and the recording is the honest one.
